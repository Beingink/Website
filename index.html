About the programme

Not merely a language school

Persianate Collective is a three-week school of Persian language, literature and culture, taught in Lucknow, India, to an international group of students.

Most language programmes begin with grammar and hope that culture arrives later, if it arrives at all.

We begin from the opposite premise: that Persian is best learned through the texts, sounds and habits of mind Persian speakers have used to think for the better part of a millennium.

A student who has recited a line of Hafez before they can order tea has learned something a textbook cannot teach.

We are not, in other words, simply a Persian language school. Grammar and script are the entry point, not the destination.

The four texts

  • GolestānگلستانSaʿdī
  • MasnaviمثنویRumi
  • RubaiyatرباعیاتKhayyam
  • GhazalsغزلHafez

Students meet all four first in translation, then, in fragments, in the original.

Manuscripts, recitation, music and the movement built to carry it, and conversations with scholars and specialists, sit alongside the texts.

Persian, on this programme, is heard and spoken as often as it is read.

The structure

Three weeks. Three worlds.

The programme follows one throughline — language into literature into world — and each week carries its own weight.

01

Language زبان

Week one builds the foundation: script, pronunciation, core vocabulary and grammar, enough to begin reading simple Persian unassisted. The aim isn't mastery. It's sufficiency — enough of the language, quickly enough, that week two has something real to work with.

02

Literature ادبیات

Week two puts that foundation to use. Students read Saʿdī, Rumi, Khayyam and Hafez with the Persian they now have, moving between the original and its translation to see what each loses and gains. The texts are read as literature, not as language drills — for their stories, their metaphors, their long argument with each other across centuries — with grammar taught through them rather than before them.

03

World جهان

Week three moves outside the classroom, into Persianate India and contemporary Iran: basic Farsi news, music and poetry heard rather than read, conversations with scholars, and field visits that place the language in the world it actually lives in. By the end of the week, Persian has stopped being a subject and started being a way of encountering things.

Woven through all three weeks

The texture of the programme

Across all three weeks, the classroom keeps expanding: Persian manuscripts handled and read; poetry recited aloud, the way it has always been transmitted; a music and movement session each week, in which students take apart a Persian song — its lyrics, its vocabulary, its cultural context — before learning or building movement to it; visiting scholars and specialists in conversation with the group; and a running thread on Persian's long history in India, alongside Farsi as it's spoken and written in Iran today. Students don't only read Persian. They hear it, speak it, and — in the music sessions — move to it.

Who it's for

Built for the seriously curious

The programme is built for international students and young adults with a real interest in Persian language and literature, in Iran and the wider Middle East, in international relations, history, South Asian studies, or art and cultural history — and for anyone drawn to the Persianate world who wants to enter it properly rather than skim its surface.

No prior Persian is required. Students who already have some exposure to the language will find enough depth here to be stretched.

What three weeks can honestly give you
